Refine the existing design.

DO NOT redesign the publication from scratch.

The current direction is strong and should be preserved approximately 70–80%.

KEEP:

- the warm paper background
- the current editorial serif / sans-serif relationship
- restrained dark text and small accent colors
- thin rules
- strong headline scale
- the overall Places page composition
- the overall Forces page
- the restrained Library index
- asymmetric editorial layouts
- the existing print-magazine feeling

The next iteration should make the publication more distinctive and less like a generic premium cultural magazine.

MAIN GOAL:

Give “The World From Below” a recognizable editorial identity.

It should feel more observational, documentary, human and intellectually curious.

Do not make it more decorative.

--------------------------------

1. INTRODUCE EDITORIAL MARGINALIA

Create a restrained recurring system inspired by printed magazine folios, archival notes, field notebooks and documentary metadata.

Examples:

PLACE 004
SHENYANG
LIAONING / CHINA

FIELD NOTE 017
02 SEP 2026

01 — WORK

1990s / NORTHEAST CHINA

These details should be very small and understated.

Use them selectively in margins, captions, section intros and article metadata.

They should become a subtle visual signature of the publication.

Do not make the site look like a database dashboard.

--------------------------------

2. INCREASE CONTROLLED ASYMMETRY

Some homepage sections currently feel too grid-like.

Especially “Explore by Force”.

Reduce the feeling of equal boxes.

Use:

different text scales,
offset alignment,
intentional blank space,
uneven vertical rhythm,
editorial positioning.

The composition may be slightly imperfect in the way a designed magazine spread is imperfect.

Avoid chaos.

--------------------------------

3. HOMEPAGE COVER SYSTEM

Design three alternative treatments for the homepage lead story while preserving the same site design system:

A — PHOTO COVER

A dominant documentary photograph with headline layered or positioned against it.

B — TYPOGRAPHIC COVER

A striking editorial question or story headline dominates without requiring a hero image.

C — SPLIT COVER

Image and headline occupy unequal halves of the composition.

These are editorial modes, not different websites.

An editor should later be able to select one in the CMS.

--------------------------------

4. VISUAL MOODS

Clarify five controlled story moods.

DOCUMENTARY
Warm paper default.
Human photography.
Standard serious editorial reading.

QUIET
More whitespace.
Narrower reading measure.
Minimal visual interruption.

ARCHIVE
Historical photography,
small dates,
source metadata,
document-like details,
occasional contact-sheet treatment.

CINEMATIC
Dark background is permitted.
Large photography or film stills.
Immersive but still readable.

MINIMAL
Designed for Field Notes.
Very little chrome.
Short observations.
Fast publishing.

Dark backgrounds must NOT become the default site treatment.

--------------------------------

5. DOCUMENTARY INTEGRITY

Do not imply that placeholder images depict actual people, places or historical events.

Remove fabricated documentary captions, author names, dates and factual details from the prototype.

Use clearly marked placeholder language where necessary.

Never label a generic image as:

“Shenyang, 1995”

unless the image has actually been verified as Shenyang in 1995.

Never invent a famous author byline.

For design mockups use:

PLACEHOLDER IMAGE
EXAMPLE CAPTION
EDITORIAL DEMO

where factual provenance is unknown.

The publication values truth over visual convenience.

--------------------------------

6. PHOTOGRAPHY ETHICS

Avoid visual tropes that exoticize poverty or hardship.

Do not use an image of a visibly poor person simply to represent:

India,
migration,
class,
poverty,
or ordinary life.

Show the full texture of ordinary life:

work,
home,
transportation,
leisure,
streets,
waiting,
families,
objects,
architecture,
routine,
friendship,
public space.

Ordinary life is not synonymous with suffering.

--------------------------------

7. LIBRARY

Keep the Library index restrained and list-like.

Do NOT make it resemble ecommerce.

But design one conceptual Library Item Detail page showing how a book or film becomes an editorial entry.

Use:

A Tree Grows in Brooklyn

Include sections:

WHY I CAME TO THIS BOOK

WHOSE WORLD DOES IT OPEN?

THE LARGER FORCES

WHAT CHOICES REMAIN?

ONE QUESTION I KEPT THINKING ABOUT

READ / WATCH NEXT

Related:
Brooklyn
Class
Family
Education

External purchase / borrow links should be visually secondary.

No star ratings.
No large Buy buttons.

--------------------------------

8. ARTICLE READING

Create a PAPER-MODE Feature Story concept.

The existing dark feature treatment can remain as the CINEMATIC variant.

Default long-form reading should use the warm paper system.

Reading width should remain highly comfortable.

Use inline imagery, captions, pull quotes and occasional full-bleed visual interruption without making the reading experience theatrical.

--------------------------------

9. BRAND RECOGNITION

Do not add a decorative logo yet.

Use typography plus the marginalia / folio system as the recognizable visual language.

The publication name remains:

The World From Below

The author remains secondary to the publication brand.
