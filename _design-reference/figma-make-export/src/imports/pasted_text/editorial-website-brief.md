Design a responsive editorial website for an independent digital publication.

WORKING BRAND NAME:
"The World From Below"

This is only a working title. Make the brand name easy to replace later.

CORE IDEA:
The publication explores how ordinary people live inside forces larger than themselves: history, work, class, migration, family, education, institutions, economic change, identity, cities, and political or social transformation.

This is NOT a news website.
This is NOT an academic journal.
This is NOT a lifestyle blog.
This is NOT an affiliate shopping site.
This is NOT a personal influencer website.

It should feel like an independent documentary magazine made by a serious reader.

The central editorial question is:

"How do ordinary people live in a world they didn't design?"

AUDIENCE:
English-speaking readers aged roughly 25–55 who enjoy serious nonfiction, literature, memoir, documentary film, history, sociology, photography, and cultural observation, but who are not necessarily academics.

CONTENT EXAMPLES:
- Chinese factory workers during the layoffs of the 1990s
- A working-class girl growing up in Brooklyn
- Migrants living in a new city
- Ordinary families experiencing economic transformation
- Jia Zhangke and the people captured in his films
- Books that let readers enter another society from the inside
- How people preserve dignity inside systems they cannot control

DESIGN PHILOSOPHY:

Create a visually sophisticated editorial experience where typography, photography, whitespace, rhythm, and content hierarchy matter more than UI decoration.

The site should feel contemporary but timeless.

Visual references:
- independent documentary magazines
- serious literary magazines
- photo essays
- beautifully art-directed print magazines
- archival books and documentary photography

DO NOT COPY any specific publication.

AVOID:
- SaaS landing-page aesthetics
- glassmorphism
- gradients
- excessive rounded cards
- pill-shaped UI everywhere
- giant CTA buttons
- generic startup illustrations
- overly cute design
- luxury lifestyle aesthetics
- beige "slow living" Instagram style
- generic masonry blog grids
- every card having the same size
- excessive animation
- decorative UI that interferes with reading

VISUAL CHARACTER:

Beautiful because real life is visually rich, not because reality has been polished into luxury.

Photography should feel:
documentary,
observational,
human,
textured,
imperfect,
cinematic when appropriate.

Possible subjects:
streets,
apartments,
factories,
train stations,
markets,
workers,
families,
hands,
old buildings,
public transport,
ordinary kitchens,
small shops,
personal belongings,
cities.

TYPOGRAPHY:

Use an expressive editorial serif for major headlines and a highly readable modern sans-serif for navigation, metadata, and body-supporting UI.

Long-form body copy must be extremely comfortable to read.

Desktop article body:
approximately 680–760px maximum text width.

Use strong contrast between:
very large headlines,
small metadata,
comfortable long-form text,
captions,
pull quotes.

Do not make typography decorative at the expense of readability.

COLOR:

Primary experience:
off-white / paper-like background
near-black text

Use restrained editorial accent colors.

Different editorial themes may have their own subtle accent, but the publication must still feel like one brand.

Create support for 5 visual moods:

1. Documentary
2. Quiet
3. Archive
4. Cinematic
5. Minimal

These visual moods should subtly influence layout, image treatment, background, typography scale, or spacing.

They must NOT create five unrelated websites.

HOMEPAGE:

Do NOT create a standard chronological blog feed.

The homepage should feel like opening a new issue of a magazine.

Design a desktop homepage at 1440px and responsive mobile version at 390px.

Homepage structure:

1. Minimal masthead/navigation.

2. Opening editorial statement.

Use:

"How do ordinary people live
in a world they didn't design?"

A short supporting line:

"Stories, books and images about ordinary lives inside history, culture and forces larger than ourselves."

Do not use a large corporate CTA.

3. Lead Feature Story.

Use a large documentary photograph.

Sample story:

WHEN THE FACTORY CLOSED

Shenyang · Work · 1990s

A story about families living through the collapse of the old industrial order.

The image and headline should dominate.

4. A deliberately asymmetric editorial section showing 3 different content types:

BOOK

These three items should NOT use identical cards.

10. Footer.

STORY SYSTEM:

The publication should support multiple story layouts so publishing does not become visually repetitive.

Create visual examples for:

ESSAY
Text-led, minimal, highly readable.

FEATURE
Large photography and dramatic editorial hierarchy.

PLACE
Immersive full-width geographic storytelling.

BOOK
Book cover + reading context + editorial notes.

FILM
Cinematic video/still-focused presentation.

FIELD NOTE
Short, fast, lightweight publishing format.

The visual system must create CONTROLLED VARIETY.

Every layout should clearly belong to the same publication while making different stories feel fresh.

ARTICLE READING UX:

Include:
reading progress
estimated reading time
excellent image captions
pull quotes
inline photography
full-width image option
image gallery option
footnotes / sources section
related stories
related places
related forces
related library items

Do not overload the reading UI.

INTERACTION:

Use subtle motion only:
image reveal,
gentle typography transitions,
editorial hover states,
small navigation transitions.

No flashy parallax.
No excessive scroll-jacking.

ACCESSIBILITY:

High text contrast.
Readable sizes.
Keyboard-accessible navigation.
Clear focus states.
Respect reduced-motion settings.

OUTPUT:

Build a polished interactive design prototype showing:

1. Homepage desktop
2. Homepage mobile
3. Feature Story
4. Essay
5. Book Story
6. Field Note
7. Places index
8. Forces index
9. Library index

Create reusable design tokens and components.

Prioritize art direction, typography, reading experience, photography and editorial rhythm.
